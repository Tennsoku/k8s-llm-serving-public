# M1.3 Review — Canonical Concurrency Sweep

## Review disposition

M1.3 is **closed** for the tested workload and range. The result is a
workload-scoped operating reference, not a general capacity claim.

## Observed facts

The following values are medians across three repetitions unless marked as a
maximum:

| Concurrency | Output TPS | TTFT p95 (s) | E2E p95 (s) | Max waiting | Max KV-cache usage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 64 | 6038.27 | 0.0778 | 0.6970 | 0 | 0.253% |
| 96 | 7215.04 | 0.0933 | 0.8685 | 0 | 0.429% |
| 128 | 7867.34 | 0.1135 | 1.0698 | 0 | 0.545% |

Across the C16/C32/C64/C96/C128 run group:

- 15 measured rows completed with zero failed or timed-out requests;
- max waiting requests remained zero;
- the prefix-cache token-hit ratio was 0.993103 in every row;
- GPU utilization peaked at 96% for every C64/C96/C128 repetition, while
  average utilization was lower at the higher concurrency levels.

## Interpretation

- Throughput continued to increase through C128, but its marginal gain was
  already declining across C64-C96. C64 is therefore retained as an efficient
  reference and C96 as a reproducible pressure reference.
- C96 is not called saturation. C128 is retained only as stress evidence.
- The progressive latency increase, zero observed waiting queue, zero failures,
  and low KV-cache usage do not establish a latency/queue knee or hard capacity
  boundary within the tested range.
- GPU utilization did not track the service transition monotonically. It is
  useful context, but it is not a saturation or capacity decision point on this
  architecture.

## Limitation and unknowns

The fixed prompt produced a 99.31% prefix-cache token-hit ratio. This sharply
reduced repeated prefill work, so the sweep does not characterize controlled
cache-miss workloads. C64/C96/C128 must not be copied to other workload shapes
or models.

The following remain unknown:

- the latency/queue knee beyond C128 for this workload;
- the hard capacity boundary for this workload;
- workload-specific operating points under request-level cache identity isolation.
